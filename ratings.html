HOW WE RATE A CARD

Methodology · version 1.0

Most card listings give you one number: the price. That tells you what a seller wants, not what a card is worth, whether it is actually selling, or whether the number is built on real evidence or a single lucky sale.

The Main Event Rating is our attempt to show the rest of it — and to show you how much we actually know, which is the part almost nobody publishes.

The five numbers

We deliberately do not reduce a card to one score. A card can have a strong market today and limited room to grow. It can have weak recent sales and excellent long-term potential. It can look attractively priced on the strength of one unreliable sale. Collapsing all of that into a single figure hides exactly the thing you would want to know.

Vault ValueWhat the card is reasonably worth today, shown as a range rather than a single figure — because a range is the honest shape of the answer. Built from verified completed sales of the same card, weighted so that closer matches and more recent sales count for more.
Market PositionWhere our asking price sits against that range, compared on the same all-in basis including shipping. Below, within, or above the estimated market.
Market HealthWhether the card is actually trading right now — sales pace, how much competing supply is listed, how stable recent prices have been, and which way they are moving.
Upside PotentialHow the card compares with similar cards on the things that tend to support future demand: scarcity, whether it is a key card, the strength of the set, demand for the person on it, and what is coming up for them.
ConfidenceHow much evidence sits behind everything above. This is the number that keeps the others honest.

Why confidence comes first

An Upside score of 85 built on one proxy sale is not the same claim as an 85 built on twenty matched sales, and it should not look the same. So confidence is displayed next to every rating rather than buried inside it, and a rating shown at lower confidence is deliberately drawn with less visual weight.

ConfidenceWhat it means
80–100 · HighSeveral recent sales of this exact card in this exact condition.
60–79 · MediumA handful of good matches, or close matches that needed adjusting.
40–59 · LowThin evidence — often a similar parallel rather than this one.
Below 40Not enough to publish. The card shows no rating at all.

This is why many cards here show no rating. A card numbered to 5 may have no recent public sale anywhere. A base card worth a dollar may have no tracked market at all. When that happens we would rather show you nothing than dress up a guess as a measurement.

Cards under about $15 are valued at the set level rather than individually, and are never given a published rating.

Where the numbers come from

  • Completed sales only. What cards actually sold for — never what someone is asking. Active listings tell you about competition, not value.
  • Matched to the exact card. Same year, set, parallel, serial numbering, autograph type and condition. A base card and a gold parallel numbered to 10 are different cards, and an on-card autograph is not a sticker autograph.
  • Weighted, not averaged. Closer matches and more recent sales carry more weight, and we use a weighted median so one unusual auction cannot drag the whole estimate.
  • Lots, reprints, damaged cards and mistitled listings are excluded.

When there is no exact sale

For very short-printed cards we estimate from the nearest comparable — a neighboring parallel, adjusted for how print run has historically affected price in that set. Any value reached that way is labeled as an estimate and carries low confidence by definition.

How often it updates

Research runs weekly on the cards where there is enough of a market to be worth tracking, and monthly on the rest. Every rating shows the date it was last researched. We also apply a minimum movement threshold before a published score changes, so a rating moves when the evidence moves, not because one sale happened to land on a Tuesday.

What these ratings are not

  • Not a prediction. A rating of 81 does not mean an 81% chance of going up. It means the card ranks strongly against similar cards on the factors described above.
  • Not investment advice. We do not publish buy, sell or hold calls.
  • Not a guaranteed sale price. Vault Value is an estimate of the market, not an offer, an appraisal, or a promise of what a card will fetch.
  • Not yet calibrated against outcomes. This is an evidence-based model, not a backtested one. We store every weekly score so that in time we can check whether high-rated cards actually did better — and publish what we find, including if the answer is no.

Ownership disclosure

Main Event Card Vault estimates are based on available market information and comparable cards. Main Event Card Vault may own or offer for sale the cards it rates. Collectible prices can change, and ratings do not guarantee future value.

We publish the confidence behind every rating, and suppress ratings we cannot support, precisely because we are rating our own inventory. If a rating looks wrong to you, it may be — tell us and we will re-examine it.
