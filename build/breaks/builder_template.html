How it works

Start with the step-by-step walkthrough, then the short version, then every section of the builder explained setting by setting.

Watch it step by step

A click-by-click walkthrough of a real pick-your-team break: four boxes of 2026 Topps Chrome Football, one giveaway team left out, priced from what you paid, then shared to your store. Each section has a note on what its settings mean.

Open the walkthrough in Scribe

1. Score every name

  • Each name earns points for every box it can come out of: base, autograph, insert, multi-person card.
  • Two boxes of the same product count twice.
  • Star multipliers scale a name up, so a Cena letter outweighs a letter full of NXT rookies.

2. Split the heavy letters

  • A fair share is the total score divided by your spot count.
  • Any letter above about 1.3 fair shares gets cut, either by second letter (SA-SH, SI-SZ) or into Men and Women, whichever evens it out better.
  • It keeps cutting until there are enough pieces to fill every spot.

3. Pack the spots

  • Best balance: biggest pieces go first into the lightest spot, then it swaps pieces between the heaviest and lightest spots until nothing improves.
  • A to Z order: finds the cut points along the alphabet that land closest to a fair share each.

4. Price it (optional)

  • Enter what you paid (or a sales goal), and each spot is priced by its share of the total score, rounded to the dollar.
  • Treat it as a starting point. Hype, rookies and case hits move real prices.

Boxes in the break

Tells the builder which names can come out of your break, and how many times.

Find and add a box

Type in "Search boxes" and the list narrows as you go. Any words work in any order: "chrome wwe", "2026 football", "sapphire" or "bowman basketball". Sports work as plain words too, so "wrestling" finds WWE and MLW, and "baseball" finds every MLB set. Newest years come first. Click a result (or press Enter for the highlighted one, using the up and down arrows to move) and it moves into your list with a counter. Clicking a box you already have adds another one. If you'd rather browse, the "Or browse by sport" dropdown below it lists everything grouped by sport.

The checklists come from the official Topps and Panini lists, and the Checklist library tab shows exactly what's in each one.

How many of each

Set the counter to the number of boxes you're actually opening. Every name's points get multiplied by that count, so two boxes of a product count twice as much as one. If a name shows up in two different products, the points from both add together.

Box type: Hobby, Jumbo, Breaker's Delight and more

Products with an official Topps odds sheet get a box type menu under the name. Pick the format you're actually opening, and the odds for that format are used. A set that isn't in that format at all (a Sapphire-only parallel, a Breaker's Delight exclusive) doesn't count, and the rest are weighed against what's typical for that box. Each box type also gets its own price line in section 5. Opening 2 Hobby and 1 Jumbo of the same product? Click "+ Add another box type" under it to get a second line (it picks Jumbo first when there is one), then set the counts. 27 products have box types so far; the rest are Hobby only.

Taking a box out

The x next to a box removes it. Your other settings stay put.

Paste your own names

For a product that isn't in the library yet, or a custom list, type or paste one name per line. Add "| W" after a woman's name so Men / Women splits work, and "| auto" after anyone with an autograph so they get auto points. The whole pasted list counts as one box.

Spots

How many spots you're selling, and whether they're cut by letter or by team.

Split spots by: Letters or Teams

Letters works for any checklist. Buyers get every name starting with their letter (or letters). Teams works for checklists that list a team next to each player: football, basketball, baseball and college products.

How many spots

Anywhere from 2 to 80. The builder figures out a "fair share" by dividing all the points by your spot count, then tries to get every spot close to it.

Letter options
  • Group letters. Best balance pairs up whatever evens things out, so light letters get bundled (Q / V / Z). Keep A to Z order only groups neighbors (E-F), which is easier to explain on a stream but a little less even.
  • Simple, Balanced or Most even. This is the trade between clean labels and equal spots. Simple keeps whole letters whenever it can. Most even is willing to cut more letters into pieces to get every spot closer to the same size. Balanced sits in between.
  • Two-letter ranges. When one letter is too big for a single spot, it gets cut at the second letter where the halves come out closest, like SA-SH and SI-SZ. Any letter over about 1.3 fair shares is a candidate. Turn this off if you never want a letter split this way.
  • Men / Women spots. For checklists that mark women (the WWE and UFC ones do). "When it helps" splits a letter into Men and Women only when that evens things out better than a letter cut, and only if the smaller side is at least about a fifth of the letter. "Every letter" splits all of them. "Never" turns it off.

Team options
  • Fewer spots than teams. Best balance mixes strong and weak teams so every spot is worth about the same, like Cowboys / Jaguars.
  • One team per spot (pick your team). Set the spot count to the number of teams. The hint under "Group teams" tells you the exact number for your boxes.
  • By division. When every box is the same league (NFL, NBA or MLB), you can sell divisions instead: AFC East, NL West and so on. The spot count is ignored in this mode.
  • Players on more than one team split their points evenly between those teams.
  • Old franchises fold into today's team: Oilers go to the Titans, Redskins to the Commanders, SuperSonics to the Thunder, Bullets to the Wizards, Bobcats to the Hornets, Expos to the Nationals and Indians to the Guardians.
  • Names with no team (some legends and checklist extras) are left out of team breaks. The hint says how many.
College / NIL players

Anyone who also has a pro team in your boxes goes to that pro team. The setting only covers players who show up with a college team alone.

  • Own spot (the default): all of them become one "College / NIL" spot. For one team per spot you'll need the team count plus one.
  • Split by letter: they're grouped by the first letter of their name (or last name, if section 3 is set that way), and each letter goes to whichever team spot is lightest. The spot shows it, like "+ college A, K (12)".
  • Leave out: they aren't in the break at all.
Leave teams out (giveaways)

Giving a team away, like a free Dodgers or Raiders spot? Tap it under "Leave teams out." It comes off the spot list, its players go with it, and the pricing spreads the break's cost over the spots you're actually selling. Results, Copy, CSV and Embed all show a "Left out" line so buyers know. Tap the team again to put it back.

Letter rule

Which letter a name belongs to. Settle this before the break and say it on stream, since it's the first thing buyers ask.

Go by first name or last name

First name is the usual for wrestling. Last name is common for team sports. With last name, suffixes like Jr., Sr., II and III are skipped, so Ken Griffey Jr. is a G. One-word names (Rikishi, Sting) use that word either way.

Nicknames on the card

Some cards print a nickname in front of the name. As printed goes by what's on the card. Real name ignores the nickname.

Always true
  • A leading "The" is skipped, so The Rock is an R.
  • Accents don't matter: Rey Fénix is an F.
  • Names starting with a number go in a "123" group.
  • In team breaks this section only shows when college players are split by letter.

What counts more

How many points each kind of card is worth. Points decide how heavy each letter or team is, which decides the spots and their prices.

Base, Auto, Insert, Multi

Points a name earns for showing up in that part of the checklist, per box. The defaults are 1 for base, 2 for an autograph set, 0.5 for an insert or relic set, and 0.5 for a card shared with other people (dual and triple cards). A name in the base set and an autograph set scores both. Raise Auto if your buyers are chasing autos; set Base to 0 if base cards don't matter to them.

Player value: Off, Normal, Strong

Turns two signals from the official lists into extra points. Stars and top rookies are in far more autograph and insert sets than role players, and the Topps pack odds show how hard each set is to pull, so a 1 per case autograph counts several times a 1 per pack insert. Panini doesn't publish odds, so their print runs (/25, 1/1 and so on) do that job for Panini and Donruss boxes. Normal is the default. Strong leans on it harder. Off scores every set the same. For products with a box type menu, the odds come from the box type you picked, and sets you can't pull from that box don't count even with Off.

Superstar boost: Off, Normal, Strong, Max

Added after breakers said stars weren't pulling away enough in pick-your-team breaks. Each player's points are raised to a power against the typical player, so a star in many hit sets counts far more than a role player, and in team breaks a team counts its best player fully and each one after a little less. On 2026 Topps Chrome Baseball, 30 teams, the Dodgers go from 5x the cheapest team (Off) to about 9x (Strong), and the middle teams spread out instead of bunching. Strong is the default.

Star names

One per line, "name | multiplier". The multiplier scales that name's points, so "John Cena | 5" makes him count five times as much. Leave the number off and it uses 3. A short name works if it's a whole word in the full name, so "Cena | 5" also catches John Cena. In team breaks, a team name works the same way: "Yankees | 2".

Paul Heyman spot (WWE breaks with Paul Heyman in them): for the breaks where Paul Heyman cards are sexy. "Break him out" gives him a spot of his own, and "How sexy is Paul Heyman?" sets its weight, from A little sexy (his normal points) up to Paul Heyman Guy approved (10x). The other spots share the rest, and your spot count stays the same.

Use it for the names buyers actually chase. One huge star can't be split across spots, so their spot may stay a little heavier, and it'll cost more.

Rhea Ripley with the default points and Player value off: base 1 + auto 2 + insert 0.5 + multi 0.5 = 4 points per box. Starred at 3: 12 points.

Pricing (optional)

Tell it what the break costs you and it prices every spot by its share of the points, so the spot with the heavy hitters costs more.

What you paid

One line for each box you picked, and a separate line for each box type, since a Jumbo or Breaker's Delight box costs more than a Hobby box. Enter the price of one box (or one case, if you're counting a case as one box) and it's multiplied by the box count from section 1.

Other costs, Fees, Profit
  • Other costs: shipping, supplies, giveaways, anything else the break costs you.
  • Fees: your platform's selling and payment fees as a share of sales. It starts at 10%.
  • Profit: what you want to make on top of your cost. It starts at 20%.
Lowest spot price

Keeps the light spots from looking too cheap. Any spot under it gets raised to it, and the difference comes off the heavier spots so the total stays the same.

Change one spot's price

Open any spot in the results and type your own price in "Price for this spot". Say the Yankees come out at $166 and you know nobody will pay that: set them to $150, and the $16 gets spread over the other spots by their weight, so the total still hits your target. That spot shows a SET tag. "Use suggested" puts one spot back, and "Reset all" in the money box clears every price you set. A price you set sticks to that spot's name (the team, or letters like SA-SH), so if a settings change gives the spot a new name, it goes back to the suggested price.

Or set a sales goal

Skip the math and type the total you want the spots to bring in. It overrides the cost, fees and profit fields.

Reading the results

Everything on the right side of the builder updates as soon as you change a setting.

The numbers across the top
  • Names, boxes, spots: what went into the build.
  • Lightest vs heaviest: the lightest spot's points as a share of the heaviest. 100% would be perfectly even, so the closer to 100%, the fairer the break.
  • Price range: the cheapest and priciest spot, when pricing is on. The money box under it shows your cost, break even, target, what the spot prices add up to and the profit after fees if every spot sells.
Weight by letter (or team)

Each bar is how many points a letter has. Highlighted bars are over about 1.3 fair shares, which are the ones that get cut. In team breaks the bars run from heaviest team to lightest.

The spot list

Each spot shows its number and label, a meter for its weight, the top names by points, its share of the total, how many names and how many have autos, plus its price. Click a spot to see every name in it, tagged AUTO or STAR.

Find a spot

The search box above the spot list narrows it to the spot holding a name or team and highlights the name. Type one or two letters ("s" or "sm") to find the spot covering them. The search at the top of the page works from any tab and links straight to the spot.

Sharing it

Copy spot list copies a plain text list for your stream notes or listing. Download CSV gives you a spreadsheet. Show every name opens every spot at once. Embed in your store gives you two codes for a product page: a plain HTML table of spots and prices (works anywhere), and an iframe of a searchable spot list hosted here, so buyers can type a player and see his spot. On Shopify, paste either one into the description using the <> Show HTML button. The codes are a snapshot, so copy them again after changing the break. Your settings are saved in this browser, so the build is still there next time.

7. Team breaks

Switch "Split spots by" to Teams. Each team gets the points of its players, so a team loaded with stars (or one you star, like "Yankees | 2") costs more.

  • Fewer spots than teams: teams are grouped so every spot is worth about the same (a strong team rides with a couple of light ones).
  • One spot per team: set spots to the team count for a pick-your-team break; each team is priced by its weight.
  • By division: for one league (NFL, NBA or MLB) the spots follow the divisions.
  • College / NIL players: anyone with a pro team goes to that team. College-only players can be their own spot, be split by the first letter of their first or last name (each letter goes to one of the lighter spots, and the spot shows "+ college A, K, T"), or be left out.
  • Leave teams out: tap any team to take it out of the break, for giveaways. It's left out of the spots and the pricing, and the results list it as "Left out."
  • Old franchises roll into today's team: Oilers to Titans, Redskins to Commanders, SuperSonics to Thunder, Expos to Nationals.

8. Quick settings

  • Hits drive the price (high-end, auto-heavy boxes): raise Auto to 3 or 4.
  • Buyers want base sets (flagship, retail-style breaks): lower Auto toward 1.
  • Inserts are the chase (case hits like Cosmic Dust or Kaboom): raise Insert to 1 or 2.
  • Ignore something completely: set its box to 0.
  • A few names everyone asks about: add 3 to 5 of them as stars, usually at 3 to 5.
  • Pick-your-team prices too flat: Superstar boost to Max. Too steep: Normal.

The score is only as good as the checklist and your star list. Breakers still make the final call on nicknames, multi-person cards and anything the checklist misses.
